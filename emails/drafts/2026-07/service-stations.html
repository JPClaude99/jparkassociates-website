What changed

California fuel excise tax rises to 63.4¢ per gallon on July 1. The CDTFA’s annual SB 1 inflation adjustment takes the gasoline excise rate from 61.2¢ to 63.4¢ per gallon (diesel moves to 48.2¢) effective July 1, 2026. Update your pump prices and your per-gallon excise split before your first July delivery, and check that the rate on your first July supplier invoice matches. We broke down what it means for stations here.

Local minimum wages go up July 1. The City of Los Angeles moves to $18.42 / hour, unincorporated LA County to $18.47, and Pasadena to $18.57. Glendale has no local ordinance and follows California’s statewide rate ($16.90), which does not change July 1. This applies to your c-store cashiers and any staff who work on-site.

For your industry

Fuel excise rate change: four things to check on July 1

The annual fuel excise adjustment is a one-day event that can create a week of reconciliation headaches if you miss it. Before the first delivery of the month:

  • Apply the new 63.4¢/gallon gasoline excise rate (48.2¢ diesel) effective July 1
  • Verify the rate on your first supplier invoice matches the new rate — discrepancies happen at month-turn
  • Update pump prices to reflect the rate change before posting
  • If your bookkeeping separates fuel excise from the pump price, update the per-gallon split in your POS or accounting system

Local notes

Minimum-wage jurisdiction follows where employees physically work, not your business license address. La Crescenta stations can fall in unincorporated LA County ($18.47/hr) or the City of Glendale ($16.90/hr, the statewide rate) depending on which side of the boundary they sit. Confirm which rate applies to each location before July 1.
