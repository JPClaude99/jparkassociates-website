What changed

Local minimum wages go up July 1. The City of Los Angeles moves to $18.42 / hour, unincorporated LA County to $18.47, and Pasadena to $18.57. Glendale has no local ordinance and follows California’s statewide rate ($16.90), which does not change July 1. Verify the exact rate for each location where your staff work before the first July payroll run — wage jurisdiction follows the worksite, not your mailing address.

The federal tip deduction is in effect for 2026. Your employees can deduct up to $25,000 of reported tips on their own returns — which means accurate W-2 tip data is worth real money to them. We covered the employer side (withholding, FICA tip credit, Form 8027) in The Ledger.

For your industry

Summer hires: set up tip reporting on day one

Every seasonal hire who clears $20 in tips a month has a reporting obligation — and you have withholding obligations built on top of it. Onboarding is the cheap moment to get this right:

  • Add new hires to your POS tip capture before their first shift
  • Walk them through the cash-tip daily report once — it takes five minutes
  • By mid-July, spot-check that reported tips look plausible relative to card tips

Local notes

Minimum-wage jurisdiction follows where the work happens, not your business address. A La Crescenta address can sit in unincorporated LA County ($18.47/hr) or the City of Glendale (state rate, $16.90/hr) depending on the exact street. If you’re not sure which rate applies to a location, send us the address and we’ll confirm it — it’s a five-minute check that can save a wage-claim headache.
