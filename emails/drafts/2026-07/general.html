What changed

Local minimum wages go up July 1. The City of Los Angeles moves to $18.42 / hour, unincorporated LA County to $18.47, and Pasadena to $18.57. Glendale has no local ordinance and follows California’s statewide rate ($16.90), which does not change on July 1. Verify the rates against your city’s official ordinance before the first July payroll run. We covered California’s wage structure in The Ledger.

Halfway through the year is the right time to check your estimates. If your income is running higher or lower than you projected in January, your Q3 federal estimated payment (due September 15) is the lever. There is no California estimated tax payment due in September — the CA schedule is 30 / 40 / 0 / 30, so the next CA payment isn’t until January. We walked through how to get the number right in The Ledger.

Mid-year check

Two things worth 15 minutes in July

You have six months of real numbers now. Two quick checks while there’s still time to act:

  • Profit vs. plan. Is revenue tracking higher or lower than expected? That changes what to pay in September. If you’re unsure, send us your year-to-date P&L and we’ll run the number.
  • CA Statement of Information. Corporations file annually; LLCs file every two years in their anniversary month. A $250 late fee is avoidable — check yours now, not at the deadline.

Local notes

Minimum-wage jurisdiction follows where the work happens, not your mailing address. La Crescenta addresses can fall in unincorporated LA County ($18.47/hr) or the City of Glendale (state rate, $16.90/hr) depending on which side of the city limit they sit. If you’re unsure which rate applies to a location, send us the address or check the boundary map we covered in The Ledger.
